OpenAI has added an Epic integration to ChatGPT Health that lets clinicians import patient data from health records, according to TechCrunch.

The company said the connection provides **read-only access** to health records. That distinction is important: the integration is designed to bring information into ChatGPT Health for clinician use, rather than allowing the product to write back to the patient record.

## What changed

The Epic integration expands ChatGPT Health’s connection to clinical workflows by allowing clinicians to access patient data from one of the most widely used electronic health-record platforms.

Based on the available details, the feature centers on importing records into ChatGPT Health. OpenAI has characterized the access as read-only, limiting the integration’s role to retrieving information rather than altering clinical documentation or records.

## Why it matters

For healthcare operators and clinical technology teams, the value of an integration like this is not simply access to an AI interface. It is whether the tool can work with the systems where patient information already resides.

Electronic health records are central to clinical operations, but information retrieval can be fragmented across notes, results and other record components. A read-only connection may make it easier for clinicians to bring relevant patient information into ChatGPT Health without manually moving data between systems.

The read-only model also establishes a narrower operational boundary. It reduces the scope of the integration compared with a system that can create, edit or submit data in Epic. That may make the initial deployment easier to evaluate, while leaving the core medical record under existing clinical and organizational controls.

## What to watch next

The key questions will be how broadly the Epic integration becomes available and how it fits into real clinician workflows. Healthcare organizations evaluating the product will likely focus on which records can be imported, how access is governed, and whether the interface saves time without creating new review or documentation burdens.

It will also be worth watching whether OpenAI expands beyond read-only access. Any move toward writing information back into electronic health records would materially change the operational and governance considerations for providers.

For now, the announcement signals that ChatGPT Health is moving closer to the systems clinicians use daily, while keeping the Epic connection limited to viewing and importing patient data.
