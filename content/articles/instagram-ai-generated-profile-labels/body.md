Instagram is tightening its approach to AI-generated influencer accounts that present fictional people as if they were real.

The platform will use a new **“AI-generated profile”** label for accounts featuring AI-generated people, replacing its prior **“AI creator”** designation. Instagram says the change is intended to make the disclosure more direct: users should be able to tell when the person depicted in a profile is not human.

The company also says it will look for profiles that do not apply the label themselves and penalize them by limiting their distribution.

## What changed

The core policy shift is not simply a new badge. It is a move from a label that could describe the account operator to one that describes the nature of the persona users are seeing.

That distinction matters. “AI creator” can suggest a human creator using AI tools, while “AI-generated profile” signals that the featured identity itself is synthetic. Instagram said it had heard from users who disliked discovering only later that an apparently human profile was AI-generated.

The policy targets a growing class of AI influencer and creator accounts, including examples such as Aitana Lopez, whose realistic images can be difficult to distinguish from conventional creator content.

## Why it matters for operators

For brands, agencies and creators using virtual talent, disclosure is becoming a distribution issue rather than just a reputational choice. An undisclosed AI persona may now face reduced reach on Instagram, potentially affecting campaign performance, audience growth and monetization.

Teams operating these accounts should review how their profiles, bios and content are structured and prepare to use Instagram’s designated disclosure. They should also avoid marketing language or account presentation that encourages audiences to assume an AI-generated person is a real individual.

For marketers considering virtual influencers, the change raises a practical planning question: whether a campaign can still achieve its intended engagement once the synthetic nature of the profile is explicit. That does not rule out AI-generated characters, but it changes the operating assumptions around authenticity, audience trust and distribution.

## A broader platform signal

Instagram’s decision reflects a platform-level recognition that realistic generative media creates a different moderation problem from ordinary edited content. The issue is not only whether an image was made with AI; it is whether an account’s identity is materially misleading.

The new label creates a clearer category for users and, potentially, for advertisers assessing where their campaigns appear. It also gives Instagram a stated basis for acting against profiles that omit disclosure.

## What to watch next

The important test will be enforcement. Instagram has said it will identify non-self-labeled AI-generated profiles and limit them, but the practical impact will depend on how consistently it detects accounts and applies those penalties.

Businesses should also watch whether the label affects engagement, recommendations or advertiser demand for virtual creators. If disclosure becomes standard across major platforms, AI influencer strategies may shift away from simulated human authenticity and toward openly fictional characters or branded digital personalities.

For now, the operational message is straightforward: if the profile’s person is AI-generated, treat transparency as a platform requirement—not an optional disclosure.
