John Ternus has made his first internal message as Apple CEO about execution, not transition.

In a memo to employees, Ternus thanked predecessor Tim Cook before pointing to what he called a “huge launch next week” that would be “phenomenal,” according to reporting by Bloomberg cited by The Verge. The timing aligns with Apple’s September 9 event, where the company is expected to unveil new iPhones.

The reported lineup could include iPhone 18 Pro models and Apple’s first foldable iPhone, potentially called the iPhone Ultra. Apple has not confirmed those products.

## Why the memo matters

A CEO’s first employee message is usually closely read for priorities. Ternus’s choice to immediately center a product launch signals continuity with Apple’s operating model: major hardware releases remain the focal point for internal coordination, customer attention and the company’s commercial calendar.

That is especially meaningful if the event introduces a foldable iPhone. A new form factor would put Apple into a category already explored by other smartphone makers, while creating a fresh test of Apple’s ability to turn a hardware shift into a mainstream premium product.

For operators and product leaders, the signal is less about the wording of the memo than the sequencing. Ternus paired a leadership handoff with an imminent delivery milestone, reinforcing that transitions at large product organizations need not pause execution.

## A launch with unusually high stakes

The annual iPhone event is routinely one of Apple’s biggest moments. But a foldable device would give this year’s launch added strategic weight. It could expand the top end of Apple’s phone portfolio and create new demands across hardware design, software interfaces, manufacturing and retail positioning.

It would also put the company’s product strategy under sharper scrutiny. A foldable iPhone would need to justify its position alongside existing iPhone models rather than simply add another high-priced device to the lineup.

Ternus also referenced “incredible products already in the works and the ones we haven’t even imagined yet,” The Verge reported. The comment offers little detail, but it frames the upcoming event as part of a broader pipeline rather than a one-off debut.

## What to watch next

The September 9 event should clarify whether Apple is entering foldables now, and how it intends to segment the iPhone range if it does. The key questions will be practical: the product’s positioning, its place relative to Pro models, and the software experience Apple builds around a flexible display.

For Ternus, the event is also an early public marker of his tenure. The launch was clearly developed before his CEO appointment, but how Apple presents its next hardware direction will shape the first external reading of the company under new leadership.

The immediate message to Apple staff is straightforward: the handoff is complete, and the next delivery deadline has arrived.
